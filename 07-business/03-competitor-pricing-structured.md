# Competitor pricing note: Structured

**Source supplied by founder:** Structured Help Center, “How to Upgrade to Structured Pro,” edited approximately three months before capture.

## Reported US pricing

| Plan | Price |
|---|---:|
| Monthly | US$6.99 (≈ £5.25) |
| Yearly | US$29.99 (≈ £22.50) |
| Lifetime | US$99.99 (≈ £75.00) |

Structured also reports:

- regional pricing based on country, currency and purchasing power
- cross-device sharing
- a three-day free trial
- subscription cancellation at least 24 hours before renewal
- an annual scholarship for users who cannot afford full price
- occasional weekly Android pricing in some countries

## What this suggests for Hold

### 1. £4.99 monthly is plausible

Structured’s US$6.99 monthly plan shows that users do pay a recurring fee for a supportive planning product. Hold, however, is likely to be used more episodically, so monthly value must be clear.

### 2. Annual pricing is strategically important

Structured’s annual price is much cheaper than paying monthly for a year. This encourages commitment while preserving a lower effective monthly cost.

For Hold, **£24.99–£29.99 yearly** is a credible test range.

### 3. Do not copy the three-day trial

Three days may be too short for an episodic product. A user might not experience a low-capacity moment during the trial. Prefer:

- 7–14 days, or
- a free tier that allows users to experience the core value before upgrading

### 4. Avoid weekly subscriptions

A weekly plan creates a high annualised cost and may feel especially uncomfortable for a product serving people during periods of illness or distress.

### 5. Scholarship access fits Hold’s values

A simple access programme could become part of Hold’s trust proposition. It should be easy to request and not require intrusive proof.

### 6. Lifetime pricing should wait

Structured can offer lifetime access because its cost model may be relatively predictable. Hold may carry ongoing AI, messaging, sync and support costs. Do not offer lifetime access until unit economics are known.

## Recommended initial Hold pricing test

| Tier | Price hypothesis | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| Free | £0 | Core Hold and return journey |
| Monthly | £2.99 or £3.99 | Introductory pricing to test |
| Yearly | £19.99 or £24.99 | Best-value plan |
| Access | Free or reduced | Scholarship/sponsored access |
| Lifetime | Not at launch | Review after cost data |

## Research questions

- Would users pay continuously for an episodic product?
- Is “pause membership” more attractive than cancellation?
- Which paid features feel valuable rather than exploitative?
- Would users prefer a one-off “support pack” purchase?
- Does annual pricing reduce financial pressure or feel like overcommitment?
