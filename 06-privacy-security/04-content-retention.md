# What personal content is kept, and for how long

Resolves the previously open "Keep this on your device for" retention window (see `08-decisions/04-open-questions.md`, now removed) and the deliberately-unplaced retention control noted on Personalise in `04-ux-content/01-core-journeys.md`.

Retention maps to whose content it is, and what it costs the user to lose — not one blanket rule.

## The three tiers — superseded 2026-08-11, kept struck through for the record

1. **Library** (saved templates) — permanent, no timer. Only ever holds the user's own words: a template they wrote or explicitly chose to save. Never contains anyone else's message. Low sensitivity by nature. **(Unaffected by the 2026-08-11 resolution below — still permanent, no timer.)**

## Draft retention windows — resolved 2026-08-11

**This is the single, intended retention policy — not a competing proposal sitting alongside the tiers above.** The day-based windows below supersede the hour-based tiers above outright; they were an earlier draft, not a separate later decision. **Implemented 2026-08-21** — `hold-app`'s own `docs/09-decision-log.md` has the technical detail.

- **Library** — unaffected, still permanent, no timer.
- **Going Quiet drafts and the Reconnect draft: 30-day retention.** Device-storage-hygiene backstop only — never framed to the user as a timer, countdown, or deadline in any copy or UI, consistent with "Disclosure, not pressure" below.
- **Conversations reply drafts (including the paired friend's-message field): 7-day retention** — shorter specifically because this record includes a third party's pasted content, deserving a tighter privacy window than the user's own words. Both fields on the same record share one lifecycle; `friendMessage` does not expire earlier than the user's own draft — they clear together, on the same 7-day clock, not on separate schedules.
- **Heads-up before auto-clear.** Before any Conversations reply draft is auto-cleared, a quiet, non-alarming heads-up appears while the app is open around that time — not a push notification, not urgency-framed. Something close to: *"This draft has been open a while. Want to send it, or save it to Library?"* **Shipped copy differs slightly, flagged rather than silently mismatched**: no "save a Conversations reply to Library" action exists anywhere in the app yet (Library's saved-template mechanism is Circle-scoped, for Going Quiet/Reconnect messages, not per-person Conversations replies — see the rule directly below, which is itself still just a rule on paper until that action is built) — the shipped heads-up instead reads "This draft has been open a while. Send it when you're ready, or keep it as is." Revisit once "Save to Library" for a Conversations reply is actually built.
- **"Save to Library" scope rule — a hard rule, not a default.** Saving a Conversations reply draft to Library saves **only** the user's own authored/edited text, **never** the friend's pasted message. **Not yet built** — there's no "save a Conversations reply to Library" action in the app at all yet; this rule governs it once it exists.

## Disclosure, not pressure

None of this is ever surfaced as a countdown, an expiry warning, or a deadline inside the flow — that would be exactly the guilt/pressure pattern Hold avoids everywhere else (see `04-ux-content/02-voice-and-language.md`'s governing voice principle). It's disclosed plainly in the privacy policy instead: drafts a person doesn't send are kept on their device for a limited time and then cleared automatically. ~~At most, a one-time, calm mention appears the first time someone saves a Personalise reply for later, framed as reassurance ("Your reply stays on your device for a couple of days in case you need to step away. Their message clears sooner.")~~ ~~("Your reply and their message both stay on your device for about a week in case you need to step away, then clear together automatically.")~~ **Finalised 2026-08-11** — at most, a one-time, calm mention appears the first time someone saves a Personalise reply for later, framed as reassurance: *"This and their message stay private to your device for about a week, then clear together. Nothing to manage."* — never repeated, never a warning.

## Lapsed/dormant backup-account retention — proposal, not confirmed, logged 2026-08-11 from a session write-up, scope corrected 2026-08-31

**Scope, corrected 2026-08-31 — this section applies to anyone with backup enabled, not Hold+ subscribers specifically.** Backup (Sign in with Apple/Google, "back up my data," covering device loss) moved from Hold+ to free-for-everyone on 2026-08-20 (`08-decisions/01-decision-log.md`); this section was originally scoped around it being Hold+-exclusive, flagged as stale on 2026-08-21, and this is that correction pass. **The retention proposal's substance below is unchanged — a dormant account is a dormant account regardless of tier** — only the framing changes: "lapsed subscription" as the trigger is replaced by **last app open**, which already works identically for free and paid users alike (both are already listed as measurement points below, this just makes free-tier the *normal* case rather than a parenthetical "if any exist under this model"). It still does **not** apply to no-login use with backup off — Hold has no required account or login for the core app either way; Circles/templates/History live on-device by default, and for anyone not using the optional backup, there is nothing server-side to warn about or delete. Their data lives and dies with their own device, outside Hold's control entirely. Distinct from the draft-content retention windows above, which apply on-device regardless of account status.

**Proposed tiered approach — not yet legally or clinically confirmed, a founder-proposed starting point only:**
- **Grace period: 30 days** before anything changes, measured from whichever trigger applies — a lapsed Hold+ subscription (for a subscriber who also has backup on) or simply crossing the dormant threshold below (for anyone else with backup on, subscribed or not) — accounts for accidental lapses/normal quiet periods without punitive immediate action.
- **Dormant window before deletion: 6–12 months**, measured from last payment (subscribers) or last app open (everyone else, now the default case, not an edge case). Chosen to genuinely serve episodic-use patterns (a resolved flare, a bad winter, a life event) rather than defeat the purpose of building for episodic need — too short risks deleting a genuinely-returning user's data right when they need it again; indefinite retention is the kind of thing a DPIA reviewer will push back on.
- **Warning before deletion — decided, not just proposed:** deletion must never happen silently; a warning has to be sent before it occurs.

**Warning delivery mechanism:**
- Push notification is the only channel confirmed to work without further verification for this group — tied to the device, no login-detail dependency.
- Sign in with Apple's private email relay is a possible secondary channel, but its exact mechanics for triggering backend-initiated communication weren't confirmed against current Apple developer documentation in the source session — needs verification before being relied on.
- **Real gap, not resolved:** if a user has deleted the app entirely (not just gone inactive within it), a push-notification-only warning can't reach them — deletion could proceed without them ever seeing the warning. Whether this is acceptable (arguably reasonable, since they've functionally already left) or justifies a fallback contact method (which would carry its own privacy trade-offs) is an open question, not decided.

**Also clarified in the same discussion, worth recording since it resolves a plausible-sounding but incorrect concern:** re-entry/renewal friction after a lapse is not actually a privacy problem — subscription/payment state is handled by Apple/Google (App Store Connect, Play Billing) directly, not stored by Hold. "Restore Purchases" (already on the Phase 10 monetisation list) is the privacy-safe re-entry mechanism, since it asks Apple/Google, not Hold's own memory. Data retention for a lapsed *backup account* (this section) is a genuinely separate decision from subscription cadence itself.

**Open questions, not decided here:**
1. Confirm the exact grace/dormant window figures (30 days / 6–12 months) with a solicitor/DPIA reviewer — proposed numbers, not validated ones.
2. Resolve the Sign in with Apple relay mechanism question against current Apple developer documentation.
3. Decide whether the "app deleted, unreachable" warning gap needs a fallback, or is accepted as-is.
