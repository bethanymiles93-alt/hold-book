# Relationship continuity landscape

**Status:** Working research summary  
**Evidence type:** Competitor and category analysis  
**Decision relevance:** Positioning, MVP scope and pricing

## Core observation

Most relationship and mental-health apps assume the user still has enough capacity to engage with prompts, tracking, exercises, games or therapy.

Hold begins at the point where that assumption fails.

The user may still care deeply, but may not be able to start or sustain a conversation. Hold therefore focuses on **communication continuity during periods of reduced capacity**.

## Competitor categories

### Relationship connection apps

Examples include Paired, Gottman Card Decks and Agapé.

They support:

- regular conversation
- relationship reflection
- structured prompts
- empathy and conflict skills
- shared rituals

Their limitation for Hold's target moment is that they still require both people to participate and respond. During a depressive episode, shutdown, grief, burnout or communication freeze, even a five-minute prompt may be too much.

### Mood and health sharing tools

Examples include Daylio and MyTherapy.

They support:

- mood logging
- symptom trends
- medication tracking
- selected sharing with another person

Their limitation is that data does not automatically communicate meaning. A low mood score may not tell a partner whether to offer space, practical help, reassurance or urgent support.

### Therapy platforms

Examples include Talkspace and BetterHelp.

They support:

- professional treatment
- therapist-led communication
- structured relationship work

They are important but operate at a very different level of cost, commitment and clinical responsibility. Hold must not present itself as a substitute for therapy or crisis support.

## The product gap

Hold addresses the period **before a fuller conversation is possible**.

It helps a user communicate four things with minimal effort:

1. My capacity is reduced.
2. My silence is not a withdrawal of care.
3. Here is what I need or do not need right now.
4. I will reconnect when I can, without promising a date I cannot guarantee.

## Strategic distinction

Relationship apps help people communicate more.

Mood apps help people track what is happening.

Therapy apps help people work through complex problems with professional support.

**Hold helps preserve the meaning of a relationship when communication temporarily stops.**

## Product implication

The MVP should not become a broad relationship programme. Its strength is a focused, low-capacity action:

- create a Hold
- review a message
- share it
- return later with help reconnecting

## Recipient opportunity

A later version may include an optional recipient guide explaining:

- what a Hold means
- what the sender has and has not asked for
- how to respect space without withdrawing care
- what generally helps
- what may add pressure

This should not diagnose the sender or tell the recipient how to manage a crisis. Any guidance must remain general, consent-based and clearly bounded.

## Research questions

- Can the core journey be completed in under 30 seconds?
- Does a Hold reduce recipient uncertainty?
- Does it reduce the sender's later guilt?
- Which phrases preserve care without creating an obligation to reply?
- When does recipient guidance feel supportive rather than intrusive?
- Do users want different messages for partners, friends, family and work?
