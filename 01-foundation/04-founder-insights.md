# Founder insights

These are lived-experience observations to validate, not universal claims.

## Core observations

- A person can think about someone constantly while remaining unable to reply.
- The longer silence lasts, the more a reply becomes a complex emotional task.
- “Just send something” can feel impossible because the message carries apology, explanation, fear and anticipated conflict.
- The pressure to explain everything correctly can prevent any communication.
- People often need permission to send a small, incomplete message.
- A reminder intended as support can become another proof of failure.
- Recovery is not a clean switch. “I’m back” may mean “I have a little more capacity.”
- Relationships can change because silence alters another person’s interpretation of character and intent.

## Product implications

- Keep the first useful action extremely short.
- Allow imperfect messages.
- Avoid demanding an end date.
- Provide optional reassurance without requiring personal disclosure.
- Make return gradual.
- Allow different levels of detail for different contacts.
- Never require a diagnosis or reason.
