# Core journeys

## Naming note

Earlier drafts of this book used "Create a Hold" and "Return from Hold" as the primary actions. Those have been superseded by the journey below. "Create a Hold" → **Going Quiet**. "Return from Hold" → **Reconnect**.

## The journey

```
Home
  ↓
Going Quiet
  ↓
Transition   (quiet acknowledgment, not celebration)
  ↓
Taking Time  (optional, repeatable — "Send an update" available)
  ↓
Reconnect
  ↓
Conversations   (per person: Quick message or Personalise)
  ↓
Reconnected   (quiet acknowledgment, once Conversations is complete)
  ↓
Home
```

**Decided:** both transition moments are real screens, not folded into existing states. This supersedes the earlier "not yet decided" status in `08-decisions/04-open-questions.md`.

- **Transition** (Going Quiet → Taking Time): a brief screen acknowledging the effort of what just happened, not congratulating in an overly cheerful way — someone may feel terrible. Example: "You've taken the first step. You've let the people who matter know you need some time. Taking time to recover isn't selfish. You don't need to earn rest." Primary button: "Begin Taking Time."
- **Reconnected** (Conversations complete → Home): mirrors the existing completion tone already used elsewhere ("The people who matter know. You can rest.") — a quiet acknowledgment, not a celebration screen. No confetti, no "Quiet Mode Ended" banner, just a brief moment before returning Home. Final copy: Heading "You're reconnected." Body "You've let the people who matter know you're here again. That's enough for today." Button "Done." Nothing more is required from the user here — it clears the Post-Reconnect state and returns to Normal.

Both screens are single-button, low-friction — they add a moment of acknowledgment, not extra decisions or extra taps beyond the one to continue.

Four emotionally distinct stages, kept separate on purpose:

1. **Going Quiet** — "I need space."
2. **Taking Time** — "I'm still here, but not ready." Optional and repeatable.
3. **Reconnect** — "I'm beginning to come back."
4. **Conversations** — "I'm ready to properly engage," at whatever pace that happens.

## Home

### Emotional goal
"I am safe here. I do not need to catch up."

### Four home states

| State | Main action | Secondary action |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | Going Quiet | — |
| Taking Time | Reconnect | Send an update |
| Reconnecting | Continue reconnecting | — |
| Post-Reconnect | Finish Reconnecting | Start a New Quiet Session |

**Reconnecting** (new, added with Reconnect's multi-select/completion-gate rewrite) appears when Reconnect's coverage gate hasn't been satisfied yet — whether the user just navigated away mid-session or force-quit and reopened the app. Resumes straight back into the picker, not a fresh start. Supporting copy mirrors Post-Reconnect's pattern: "Pick up where you left off," with progress ("2 of 5 reached") when available.

**Post-Reconnect** appears once the coverage gate is satisfied and the user has entered Conversations but hasn't yet addressed everyone on the list (whether by Quick message or Personalise). It does not reset to Normal. Supporting copy: "Continue where you left off," optionally with progress ("2 of 5 replies sent"). Never use "outstanding," "incomplete," "overdue," "pending," or "you still need to."

**"Finish Reconnecting" (2026-08-13) resumes the same Reconnect screen, not a separate destination.** Top to bottom: the instant-message area starts collapsed (nothing left to send to the original audience); the circle row shows already-reached Circles with the standard dark-green/white sent fill, and any Circle added to the audience *after* completion (e.g. via "Add to Going Quiet") in a third, distinct paler shade, grouped at the end of the row rather than interspersed; the "Want to reply to anyone properly?" Personalise prompt is open by default; the OOO/status ("Wider World") section sits collapsed-but-reopenable beneath it. **Confirmed collapsed-by-default 2026-08-13, superseding the 2026-08-12 entry that had deliberately defaulted it open for this same moment** — that entry is superseded, not wrong, a legitimate earlier decision point later revisited, same convention this book uses elsewhere for same-topic reversals. **New: a one-time exit nudge, not a default-open section.** Rather than opening this section every time someone returns (too much clutter) or letting it go silently unaddressed, leaving the Reconnect screen (tapping back — the only path, since swipe-back is now unconditionally disabled here, see "Bottom nav visibility" below) checks whether it's genuinely unresolved (OOO still on and not turned off, or wider-world status still active and not cleared) and, if so, offers a brief "Want to loop in/update the Wider World before you go?" prompt with an option to open it or leave anyway — never a hard block. Fires once per leave attempt, not on every re-entry, so the main resumed view stays consistently simple. Checks the real per-platform takedown checklist (2026-08-21) — every platform marked posted-to must be checked off for Status to count as resolved, not just the one combined toggle this used to check before the checklist itself existed.

The **Conversations** destination is separate from Post-Reconnect and always available, independent of whether the user has gone quiet at all — it's for getting help with a specific reply at any time, not only after a Hold journey.

### Circle text hierarchy — previously undocumented

The main interaction circle carries a two-level text hierarchy in both its states, not a single line:

- **Normal (Going Quiet available):** "Going quiet" as the bold/larger in-circle header, "Tap to let your people know" as the smaller subtitle beneath it.
- **Taking Time (Reconnect available):** "Reconnect" as the bold/larger in-circle header, "Tap when you're ready" as the smaller subtitle beneath it.

This was a decision made early on but never actually written into this book, which is why it was missed in the first build — "Tap when you're ready to reconnect" as a single undifferentiated line is the bug this corrects.

### Avoid
- activity feeds
- overdue tasks
- streaks
- social comparison
- busy dashboards
- unread counts or badges on Conversations

## Going Quiet

**One single scrollable screen, no separate Review step.** Send is the one urgent, essential action, done immediately — everything else (going deeper on a specific person, email out-of-office, wider-world status) is a calm, optional unwind that happens *after* Send, never gating in front of it. This mirrors the shape Reconnect already uses: essential action first, "want to do more?" second.

Top to bottom:

**Queue-based redesign (2026-08-11) — supersedes the generalised-All-only version of this list below, from earlier the same day.** That version was right about the core mechanic (any selected subset shares one message, one Send) but treated each selection as a one-off. On-device, this needed to become session-scoped: pick your Circles once, keep going back to the same row for as long as the session lasts, with the app knowing when everything's actually been covered rather than requiring a manual "I'm finished" tap every time.

1. **Who needs to know?** — choose one or more Circles from the full chip row. Selecting a Circle does two things at once: it joins the *current* group (sharing the message box below with whatever else is selected) and it joins the session's **queue** — every Circle ever selected this session, which only grows, never shrinks (deselecting a Circle for the current message doesn't drop it from the queue). The full row stays visible and scrollable throughout — there's no separate "reopen selection" screen; an unqueued Circle is tapped into the queue the same way any Circle is selected. **Circles currently selected float to the front and everything else greys out, but only at the one moment of moving from selection into the text box to start composing (corrected 2026-08-11)** — not recomputed on every individual tap, so selections can be freely adjusted beforehand with nothing reordering or dimming underneath the person's finger. Circle setup happens inline here ("+ New Circle" available on this screen; full management lives in Settings); a Circle created here stays local to this session until Reconnect asks whether to make it real. **Delivery, corrected 2026-08-11: individual by default** (one separate message per recipient, no one in a Circle sees who else received it) — a Circle can be switched to one shared group message instead via a toggle on the Circle itself (at creation, or in its own settings under Manage Circles), default off; a combination with mixed per-Circle settings still sends as one action, each Circle just following its own setting within it. **Resolved 2026-08-12 — N compose-sheet confirms, accepted, not a bug to fix further:** `expo-sms` has no silent-send capability (flagged when this delivery model was first corrected, see `08-decisions/01-decision-log.md`, 2026-08-11), so an individual-delivery Circle of N people means N separate native SMS compose-sheet confirmations per Send, one per recipient — investigated and settled, not an open issue. Each sheet arrives pre-filled with recipient and message already populated, so this is N single confirming taps, not N full compositions, and it doubles as a genuine final check before each message actually sends. The per-Circle "send as group" toggle already described above is the accepted mitigation for Circles where N taps would be genuinely burdensome — individual/BCC-style stays the default for small, close Circles where the extra care is worth it; group delivery is an explicit opt-in for larger ones. See the decision log for the alternatives considered and rejected (Android-only silent send via default-SMS-handler status; a server-side SMS gateway).
2. **A Circle's member list is never shown by default — only via its own dropdown arrow, one at a time.** Selecting a Circle for the queue/current message says nothing about wanting to see or adjust who's in it; that's a separate, optional action, and the two are fully independent — the arrow works whether or not that Circle is currently selected (confirmed/fixed 2026-08-19, correcting a bug where the arrow silently did nothing for an unselected Circle). Tapping a Circle's arrow reveals its recipient list beneath the row; tapping another Circle's arrow closes the first and opens the new one — never two open at once. Inside the open dropdown:
   - A **"+"** control, pinned first (matching the same "+ goes first" convention as the row's own top-level "+ New Circle"), glyph-only, styled as a small content-hugging pill rather than a large circle within this row (2026-08-19, scoped to person-pill rows specifically — the top-level Circle-picker row's own "+" stays a true circle) — adds a new member to this Circle via the native contact picker, the same mechanism Settings' Manage Circles already uses, regardless of whether the Circle is currently selected. A Circle created via the top-level "+" opens its own dropdown immediately so a second (or third) member can be added the same way, right after creating it with the first.
   - Every current member as a **small, content-hugging pill in a horizontal scrolling row beside the "+"** (2026-08-14, corrects the earlier vertical selection-circle list to match the same `AdaptiveCircleChip`/horizontal-row convention Reconnect and Library's own per-person rows already use; sized to fit the name rather than the larger standard Circle-chip diameter, 2026-08-19), any emoji in a contact's saved name stripped before it displays — tap a pill to exclude that person, single tap, no second step. Excluding someone here removes them from this Circle's list entirely — they move to the removed-people row at the top of the screen instead (point 5), and **separately, appear in a new excluded-recipient line directly above this pill row** (2026-08-20) — everyone currently excluded from any *selected* Circle, merged into one line regardless of which Circle's dropdown happens to be open, so it's clear at a glance who's no longer included for this flow. Distinct from the removed-people roster: that one is unscoped and persists regardless of selection (still the "+" bundle action's own source); this line is scoped to what's currently selected and read-only, just visibility. Anyone already bundled into a provisional Circle shows "✓" in this line, not removed from it. **If the Circle isn't currently selected, its pills are a read-only preview instead** (plain, non-interactive name labels, 2026-08-19) — there's no "current send" for a person to be excluded from yet; "+" still works normally either way. **Exception: a Circle with only one contact never shows a removable pill at all**, just their plain name beside the "+" — excluding your only recipient already has the same effect as not selecting the Circle. **A provisional Circle bundled from the removed-people roster gets its own distinct border treatment** (colour + border, never colour alone, 2026-08-20) marking it as temporary/not yet a considered grouping, and a first-time explainer the first time anyone is ever excluded.
3. **The shared message box — one per current group, not one per Circle.** A Circle-combination previously sent to auto-loads its own saved default; a brand-new combination offers **starting points** instead (one per member Circle with its own saved single-Circle default, "start from Work's message," tappable) rather than auto-loading anything, carrying the previous group's edited text forward if none is picked. No saved default and no prior text falls back to the intent chips, same as always. Single-Circle saving is unchanged (auto-persists on the first pristine pick, "Save to Library" for edits); a combination's own default saves silently, automatically, the moment a message is actually sent for that exact combination.
4. **Send.** Tapping the docked bar's own send icon dispatches immediately — no intermediate "back to preview" step, since fewer taps between done-typing and sent matters directly here. Beneath the message box, one row holds **Template (left) — Send (centre) — Save (right)** (2026-08-19, Send joining what used to be a separate row below, confirmed layout before building): Template and Save stay plain text links, Send is the app's one standard filled/primary-coloured send button, kept visually distinct even sharing the row since it's the one irreversible action of the three. "Save to Library" shortened to just "Save" ("✓ Saved" once saved) — covers sending an unedited, already-loaded default without ever opening the keyboard. Only a Circle that actually had at least one included recipient in this particular send is marked sent. **After a successful Send, the selection clears back to empty**, and the area beneath the row shows the text that was just sent, with "Save" (single Circle) or a confirmation that it's already saved as a template (a combination) — until the next Circle is tapped, which clears this and resumes normal composing. A Circle already sent to is never locked — reselecting it is a normal part of the loop; its chip fills in (the app's one standard sent treatment: dark-green fill, white text, colour only, no glyph — see the note on Reconnect's own chips below) to show "sent at least once this session," and un-fills the moment it's back in the current selection.
5. **Removing someone doesn't open Personalise — it moves them into an ad-hoc Circle-creation flow instead.** Personalise (the rich per-person Send/Edit/Personalise accordion — see `04-navigation-architecture.md`'s "Personalise pattern" section) stays Reconnect-only: it makes sense there because there's an incoming message to reply to, and Going Quiet has no equivalent history to personalise against — the heavier mechanic would just add cognitive weight at the moment someone has the least capacity for it. Instead: anyone removed from any Circle's dropdown this session (point 2) appears in a plain, comma-separated horizontal list directly below the Circle row (corrected 2026-08-11 — the Circle row must always stay topmost) — accumulating across multiple original Circles, not scoped to one. A **"+"** beneath it bundles either specifically-tapped names from the list, or — nobody tapped — everyone in the list not already claimed. The new Circle joins the queue and current selection immediately, gets its own message and Send like any other Circle. Someone already claimed by an earlier bundle shows in a visually distinct, muted state (sage rather than the normal treatment) rather than disappearing from the list — tapping them re-selects them for a *further* bundle, which is a deliberate action, not an accidental duplicate to guard against; their earlier message isn't cancelled by being re-added. As with point 1's provisional Circles, Reconnect asks whether to make each of these real later, and that prompt fires regardless of whether anything was ever sent to it — removing someone from their original Circle already put them at risk of falling out of tracking with no signal anywhere, whether or not a bundle Send ever happened.
6. **Auto-complete — no manual tap required for the normal case.** Once every Circle in the queue has been sent to at least once, the flow completes automatically: out-of-office/status activate if enabled, and it moves on to the Transition screen, exactly as if "Done" had been tapped. **"Done" itself is now specifically the early-exit action** — stopping before every queued Circle is covered — sized and positioned as a small button beside Send (corrected 2026-08-11 — not in line with the "Change template"/"Save" links, which sit above it; a stale/duplicate full-width render of Done reappearing after navigating back from Your Circles was reported and is most likely a Fast Refresh/stale-bundle artifact rather than a logic bug, since only one Done exists in the code either way). Reachable from the very start, before anything at all has been sent, the same as always — someone who decides not to send anything this session still needs a way out.
7. **Email out-of-office, then "Set your status elsewhere"** — appear once anything has been sent, as a collapsed-by-default **"Wider World"** section, immediately above Done (renamed from "OOO and status," 2026-08-21), expandable to review/set before completing (confirmed 2026-08-11 as already matching the "reveal on demand" pattern point 2's dropdown uses) — addressing it or explicitly leaving it collapsed both count as "dealt with"; completing (via Done or auto-complete) always reaches this point before the Transition screen, never skips past it unseen. **Ordering corrected 2026-08-30**, against a real bug found and fixed 2026-08-29 (`hold-app`'s own `docs/09-decision-log.md`): `doneButton` was briefly rendering twice, both times *before* Wider World, which this line's original "beneath Done" wording actually described; the fix consolidated it into one render and moved it after Wider World, to the very end of the screen — Done is now the last thing on the page, not Wider World. This line previously had that backwards.

Suggested intents:
- I'm unwell
- I need some quiet
- Feeling a little overwhelmed
- Write my own

The Going Quiet message is the initial message and should never be described as an "update."

Example: "I'm not feeling well and need to take some time. I'll be in touch when I can."

## Taking Time

The resting state. No pressure, no default reminder to reconnect.

**Send an update** (optional, secondary, repeatable): lets the user reassure their Circles during a long quiet period without ending Taking Time or moving toward Reconnect.

**Rebuilt as a bottom-sheet drawer, 2026-08-13** (supersedes the earlier full-screen-push description): a drawer slides up from the bottom, filling just over half the screen, dismissed by tapping anywhere above/outside it (same mechanic as the Settings side drawer — checked directly against its code, adapted from a side slide to a bottom slide). Title at top, a compact always-available Send button beneath it (same pattern as Going Quiet's own). Circle row uses true circles (not pills) with each Circle's own dropdown arrow revealing its people, matching Going Quiet/Reconnect's established pattern rather than the earlier pill-row design. One shared text box for whichever Circles are currently selected, unchanged in spirit — a genuinely different message to a different Circle still means a separate selection, not multiple boxes open at once. Each Circle gets its own saved default (reusing the same per-Circle/per-combination template mechanism Going Quiet and Reconnect already have, in a separate namespace so the three kinds of message can each remember their own default); a first-ever combination offers each member Circle's own single-Circle default as a starting point rather than auto-loading anything. Only shows Circles already in the tracked audience — no "+" here; extending who's included is exclusively Add to Going Quiet's job, below.

**Grouped/linked Circles:** Circles that have previously received one combined message together show visually interlinked (overlapping circles, Olympic-rings style, connected by a line) rather than as separate chips — tapping the linked cluster selects every member Circle at once. A "Group"/"Ungroup" toggle appears once a linked cluster is selected, letting the person separate them back into individually-selectable Circles for this visit without losing the saved combination itself. Each Circle's own dropdown arrow keeps working independently regardless of grouping. Here, "previously received" means a saved template exists for that exact combination — permanent, the same way any other saved default is.

**Extended to Going Quiet, Reconnect, and Conversations, 2026-08-21 — a different trigger and scope from the Update drawer's own, described just above.** Going Quiet's own combined sends (two or more Circles sent one message together) now create the same kind of link, using the identical shared Olympic-rings component and Group/Ungroup toggle — but **period-scoped, not permanent**: a link only lasts for the current Hold period, and a new Going Quiet starts with no inherited links even if the same Circles are combined-sent again later. This link then carries forward within that same period: Reconnect's instant-message screen shows the linked Circles grouped by default, with its own Group/Ungroup toggle in the same position (between the circle row and the recipient list); Conversations, which has no per-Circle selection row of its own to hang a toggle on, instead reflects whatever grouped/ungrouped choice was left at Reconnect's stage by keeping that cluster's people adjacent in its own list rather than interleaved with everyone else. Going Quiet itself never displays a cluster — it only ever creates the link, on the same send that closes it out.

Circle states: default (untouched), currently selected (a ring, regardless of whether a draft exists), sent (dark-green fill, white text — only after an actual send this session, never merely because a draft exists; deselecting a draft-only Circle returns it to default, never implying a send that didn't happen).

Sending switches each just-updated Circle to the sent-state fill without reordering it; a Circle already marked sent can still be reselected for a further update, its sent look stepping aside while selected. A Circle's "already sent" state persists across re-opening the drawer within the same Taking Time period, resetting once a new Going Quiet round begins.

Example: "I'm still taking some time and can't properly message yet, but I'm thinking of you."

**Not yet decided:** whether ungrouped audience members (people not in any Circle) should be reachable from this drawer at all — not built either way as of 2026-08-13; see `08-decisions/04-open-questions.md`.

**Add to Going Quiet** (edge case, low-key, not a prominent button): if someone new messages while the user is away, this adds them to Hold's tracked list.

**Built as a drawer, 2026-08-13, matching Going Quiet's own screen pattern rather than Send an Update's.** The contact picker opens first and is mandatory — nothing else happens if it's cancelled. The picked contact becomes their own new Circle, auto-named by first initial ("P") — confirmed as the app's existing design (every Going Quiet contact becomes a Circle, even a Circle of one; there's no separate "individual, non-Circle" category) rather than a new invention. The drawer then opens: standard circle layout, "+"/"All" pinned first, each Circle's dropdown arrow revealing its own pill row (also "+"/"All" pinned first, even for one person), then the text box. Circles added this way accumulate across the current session — several "Add to Going Quiet" taps in a row all show together in the one drawer. Circle *creation* alone doesn't add anyone to the tracked audience; only an actual Send does, matching "sending here adds to the ongoing tracked audience."

Home's older ungrouped-adding quick-add mechanic — this drawer's own precursor — is fully replaced, not left dormant alongside it. **Flagged, not resolved:** Reconnect's own separate "+" (a mid-flow add, genuinely different from this drawer since it targets a period no longer open by the time Reconnect is on screen) still adds the person ungrouped, not as a Circle, contradicting the confirmed Circle-of-one design this drawer now follows. Whether it should be brought in line, or is deliberately different because it's a different moment in the journey, isn't decided; see `08-decisions/04-open-questions.md`.

**Going Quiet again, mid-Conversations** (distinct edge case): a user may need to go quiet again before finishing the previous round of Conversations. A small "Going Quiet" action stays available on the Conversations screen itself for this. When tapped, it does not return to Home first and does not ask the user to tap Going Quiet twice — it goes straight into a brief, auto-advancing transition (with a "Skip" option) and then directly into "Who needs to know?" Example transition copy: "We're glad you're here. Keeping your circles in the loop can ease worry, reduce misunderstanding and make reconnecting easier when you're ready." This keeps momentum for someone at very low capacity who might otherwise abandon the flow. Old unfinished Conversations remain saved rather than being cleared or lost — they stay available to pick back up whenever the new quiet period ends.

**Resolved — visibility timing:** "Send an update" is hidden on the calendar day the Going Quiet message was sent, and becomes visible from the next calendar day onward — simpler and more predictable than a session-based (close-and-reopen) trigger, which would behave oddly for someone who keeps the app backgrounded for days.

**Resolved — nudge default:** on by default, not opt-in, with an adjustable interval — an opt-in-only nudge mostly gets used by people who already knew to look for it, which undercuts the point for the people it would help most. What actually protects against frustration isn't the default, it's the delivery: this is a soft **in-app** prompt shown when the person happens to open Hold, never an OS push notification pinging their phone unprompted. Copy should explicitly mention "one tap to reassure, nothing else required" so the low effort is stated, not assumed. Honest caveat: no real interval has been researched yet — treat any specific number of days as a testable hypothesis, not evidence-based, until real usage data exists.

A future, optional gentle nudge may appear after a long quiet period: "You've been taking time for a while. Would you like to send a quick update? One tap, nothing else required." with "Send update" / "Not now" — never a countdown, never guilt-based wording. Framed as an invitation, not a reminder — "Would you like to reassure your circles?" rather than "It's been 5 days." The interval is user-adjustable in Notifications settings, not fixed.

## Reconnect

**Revised — single tap, symmetric with Going Quiet.** Tapping Reconnect is not followed by an upfront "how would you like to reconnect?" choice screen. It triggers a brief transition (acknowledgment, not a question — see below) and goes straight into Conversations. This keeps Home's two primary actions symmetric (both single taps), and moves the actual decision-making to where it's already needed: per person, inside Conversations.

**Transition copy:** "Welcome back. Here's who's waiting to hear from you. Reply however feels right today."

### Audience

Reconnect persists the audience chosen at Going Quiet by default — there is no full re-pick screen. The user can still add someone new (e.g. someone who messaged while the user was away, via "Add to Going Quiet"); the full audience — every Circle and every ungrouped individual — is what the picker below reflects.

**Revised: no separate "remove someone already dealt with" escape hatch.** The completion gate below requires everyone in the audience to be reached by an instant message at least once before Reconnect's second stage unlocks — there's no way to mark someone as handled outside Hold (e.g. a phone call) and skip them. This supersedes the earlier allowance; flagged here since it's a real capability loss, not just a rewording, in case it needs revisiting.

### Reconnect screen — multi-select picker with a completion gate

Replaces the previous three-action (Send/Edit/Personalise) single-audience-box screen. Structure:

1. **Two rows, corrected 2026-08-13: a Circle-browsing row, and a shared per-person pill row beneath it** — not one flat picker. The Circle row is which Circles to draw people from, not a send-selection; tapping a Circle's chip bulk-toggles all its own people between included/excluded, its separate arrow shows/hides its people in the pill row below, independent of inclusion. Ungrouped individuals are always visible in the pill row directly. **"+"/"All" pinned at the front of *both* rows** ("+" outside the scroll, "All" first inside it) — an explicit app-wide convention as of 2026-08-13, matching Going Quiet's own Circle-picker exactly, not a Reconnect-specific placement; "+" adds a person to the audience in both rows, "All" toggles the whole audience from the Circle row vs. only what's currently visible from the pill row — a deliberate, non-redundant split. Tapping into the message box (not any earlier selection tap) is the one moment the pill row reorders — included-at-that-moment pills float to front and everything else greys out; freely adjustable before that point. **"+" now creates a real provisional Circle-of-one (2026-08-20), not an ungrouped contact** — closes a previously-flagged inconsistency (this used to be the one place in the app that didn't follow the confirmed Circle-of-one convention). That Circle gets a distinct **double-line border**, marking it as newly added mid-Reconnect, not part of the original Going Quiet audience — since this person never received a Going Quiet message and doesn't know time has passed, a dedicated apology-wording suggestion pill ("Sorry I wasn't able to let you know, I've been taking some quiet time.") is offered alongside the docked bar's own suggestion pills whenever a double-bordered Circle/person is being messaged — an available option, never the default wording, offered wherever this state can appear (Reconnect's own compose and reply boxes, and Conversations').
2. **A passive excluded line beneath the shared pill row (2026-08-20), and two available actions on it — neither forced at unselect time.** Unselecting a pill removes that person from the current send and nothing more; they simply appear on this line, same visibility-only pattern as Going Quiet's own excluded-recipient line. Tapping an entry marks it; two actions then act on whatever's marked (or everyone not yet covered, if nothing's marked): a "+" bundles the marked people into one new shared provisional Circle (useful when there's more capacity than Going Quiet's own one-at-a-time flow assumes, and several excluded people should get one shared instant message instead), and "Already told them" marks the same selection as covered without a send — satisfying the same completion requirement a real send does, without one. Someone already covered either way shows "✓" on this same line, inheriting the app's existing sent-chip treatment.
3. **One shared message box** for whichever are currently included, with its own **"Change template"/"Save" controls** underneath (2026-08-13, previously missing) — mirrors Going Quiet's own pattern: a Circle (or Circle-combination) with a saved default auto-loads it; "Save" persists the current text against whichever Circles are currently contributing; "Change template" resets to the built-in fallback and reopens the field. See "Reconnect's own saved templates" below for the underlying architecture.
4. **Send — individual by default, corrected 2026-08-11; sole send mechanism corrected 2026-08-13.** Delivery is one separate message per recipient by default (no one in a Circle sees who else received it, or that it was sent to anyone else at all — people in the same Circle may not know each other), not one shared thread. A Circle can be switched to send as one shared group message instead, via a toggle on that Circle itself (available both when creating it and from its own settings in Manage Circles), default off. A combination of Circles with mixed settings still sends as one action — each Circle just follows its own setting within it. Each just-reached Circle/individual's pill switches to the existing sent/checkmark treatment in place. Repeatable across as many separate sends as needed to reach everyone. **Same N-compose-sheet-confirms behaviour and rationale as Going Quiet's delivery section above (resolved 2026-08-12) — not repeated here.** **The separate, always-available screen-level Send button is removed (2026-08-13)** — the docked bar's own send icon is now the only way to send on this screen; "Done" (below) is exit-only, never a send trigger. Going Quiet's own equivalent button was deliberately left untouched pending its own answer on this (see `08-decisions/04-open-questions.md`).
5. **Coverage gate, no visible count (corrected 2026-08-11 — the "X of Y reached" line contradicted the app's own no-badges-no-counts rule and is now removed, not replaced with different phrasing):** until every Circle and every ungrouped individual has been reached at least once, the picker/box/Send loop is the only thing on screen — no Personalise, no "want to reply properly," nothing else competing for attention before the essential thing is actually done. **"I've already told them" (point 2 above) and a real send both satisfy this gate identically** — reaching full coverage completes the flow automatically, same trigger/model as Going Quiet's own auto-complete; a manual **"Done"** button is always available once at least one message has gone out — the one exit control on this screen, not a send trigger.
6. **Once fully covered, or once nothing's currently selected to send:** first, an **optional rename opportunity** (2026-08-13, replaces the old yes/no "Add [name] to [Circle] permanently?" gate entirely — see "Bundled Circles are real immediately" below for why), then "Want to reply to anyone properly?" → **Personalise** / **Not now** (returns to Home, unchanged). **Personalise, corrected 2026-08-11: opens the same rich per-person Send/Edit/Personalise accordion in place** (see `04-ux-content/04-navigation-architecture.md`'s "Personalise pattern" section) — the earlier plain nav-away into Library was itself the gap, not a pattern worth preserving; nothing needs seeding into Conversations here specifically, since Reconnect's own Send already does that for the whole audience regardless of this choice. Also newly unlocked here: the real **Email**/**Status** parallel structure described in full under "Wider World" below, shown only if either was turned on at Going Quiet's "Done" step — Email is real (provider-configuration-dependent, see that section), Status surfaces a real per-platform taken-down checklist rather than one combined "Clear my status" link, wherever any platform was actually marked posted-to at Going Quiet.

**Bundled Circles are real immediately, corrected 2026-08-13 — no more permanence gate.** A Circle spun out of removed people during Going Quiet (see Going Quiet's own section above) used to sit as a provisional stand-in until Reconnect asked a yes/no "should this exist" question. That question is gone: the moment Reconnect loads, any still-pending bundled Circle is made real unconditionally, with an auto-generated placeholder name (initials of its members — "P & A" for two people, "P, A & J" for three or more; a bare initial for one). This placeholder *is* the Circle's real, final name unless the person changes it — there's no separate existence decision left to make, and it can always be renamed later via Manage Circles regardless of whether the step below is engaged with. The **optional rename opportunity** (point 6 above) offers to change it, or to explicitly confirm leaving the placeholder as-is (shown next to Hold's own circle glyph, not a generic star) — either choice is final, not asked again. It appears once instant messages for the round are done and before the Personalise choice, **regardless of whether that particular bundled Circle was actually messaged this session** — so one that was never reached still gets its moment, rather than silently never being offered a name at all.

**"Not now" still returns to Home, not straight to Reconnected**, unchanged from the prior resolution: the instant messages alone don't mark anyone in Conversations complete, so Home resolves to Post-Reconnect ("Finish Reconnecting" available) rather than a forced acknowledgment. Reconnected only ever fires from Conversations/Library's own completion check.

**Force-quit resilience, by design, not an afterthought.** Coverage (who's been reached) is stored on the Hold period record itself, not just in-memory app state — closing the app mid-Reconnect and reopening resumes exactly where it left off, with Home showing "Continue reconnecting" (unquantified — "Pick up where you left off," corrected 2026-08-13, the count removed for the same no-badges-no-counts reason as the in-flow line above) rather than a misleading Normal state. See `docs/03-privacy-model.md` for what's stored and why.

## Conversations

**Revised again — two separate dropdowns, not one progressively-revealing section.** The previous version nested per-person instant messages inside the same area as the bulk flow. This version splits by *purpose* instead: a **Quick message** dropdown for the low-effort bulk case, and a **Personalise / Conversations** dropdown for anyone who needs actual attention — someone moves from one to the other by being unticked, rather than gaining a third nested level within the same section.

**The screen is organised around "who, then how"** — selection answers *who*, the two dropdowns answer *how*.

Available both as where Reconnect leads and as a standalone destination at any time, without needing to have gone quiet first.

### Dropdown 1 — Quick message (top)

**Default view — two elements only:** a scrollable Circle selection row mirroring Going Quiet's picker ("All" first, then each individual Circle), and one shared message box beneath it with one Send action. That's the whole dropdown for someone happy to send the same quick message to everyone.

**When "All" breaks** (any Circle deselected): reveals one row per Circle, all of them, each with its own message box pre-filled from a copy of the shared box (or that Circle's own saved default if one exists), independently editable, its own Send action, and a down-arrow to expand into its people.

**Unticking someone from a Circle's group removes them from Quick message entirely — they move to Dropdown 2**, not into a nested individual instant-message box within this dropdown. Quick message's whole purpose is the low-effort bulk case; anyone who needs individual attention belongs in the other dropdown, not a third tier bolted onto this one.

**Sent-state pill treatment:** once a Circle's message is sent, its pill softens and desaturates to a paler, quieter version of its own colour, paired with a small checkmark — and **loses the outer selection halo/border**, since "sent" is its own distinct state, not the selection indicator layered underneath it. Never a full colour inversion (white fill/dark green text is already assigned to secondary navigation/add-manage actions elsewhere).

**Status label evolves over time, not a single frozen state:** immediately after sending, the label reads "Instant message sent." From the next calendar day onward, it changes to show the actual date instead (e.g. "Sent 26 Jul") — "sent" alone becomes ambiguous the longer Taking Time continues, but is perfectly clear in the moment it happens.

**Sent is reopenable, not a permanent lock:** tapping a sent Circle's pill (or, when "All" is fully sent, the shared "Sent to everyone" summary in its place) reopens the same message box and Send action for another round, rather than leaving no way to message that Circle or everyone again for the rest of the Conversations session. A repeat send updates the status label's timestamp to the new send.

### Dropdown 2 — Personalise / Conversations

Contains: anyone unticked out of a Quick message group, anyone added via "+ Add person," and is also the standalone destination reachable independent of having gone quiet at all — someone can open it because they have one hard message to reply to, with nothing else required first.

**Build note:** the standalone "reply without having gone quiet" entry point is intentionally not wired up yet — it's meant to live inside Library (`04-ux-content/04-navigation-architecture.md`, "Library screen structure"), which hasn't been built. This isn't a regression; there's deliberately no route to it until Library exists.

Per person:
- **Personalise** — expands as an accordion panel directly beneath that person's row, not a separate screen. Only one panel open at a time. Panel contents: "What they sent" paste box; "Starting point" as a compact horizontal row of chips (Keep it brief / Acknowledge the wait / Explain a little / Write my own); "Your reply" text box, pre-filled from the chosen starting point, editable; send-now/save-for-later actions. Closing the panel collapses it back to a compact status row. **Retention, migrated to the resolved policy 2026-08-21:** "What they sent" and "Your reply" now share one 7-day lifecycle and clear together — the friend's message no longer clears separately or sooner, and there's no longer a separate "app fully closed" trigger for it. A quiet, one-time heads-up ("This draft has been open a while...") appears while the app is open as that window nears its end, itself never a countdown or urgency framing. See `06-privacy-security/04-content-retention.md` for the full resolved model (Going Quiet/Reconnect drafts get a separate 30-day window, unaffected by this Conversations-specific pairing).
- tick/untick as replied — always reversible, never a hard "done"
- "Conversation complete" covers: replied in Hold, replied elsewhere, phone call, saw them in person, or no further reply needed

**Post-send state.** After a Personalise reply is sent, the row's status label reads "Sent. They'll hear from you properly." (same next-day-becomes-a-date behaviour as the instant message, and same voice principle — warm, brief, no praise). Sending does not automatically tick the conversation as complete — that stays a separate, deliberate action, since "Conversation complete" already covers real-world completion paths beyond "Hold sent something."

### Adding someone new

"+ Add person," in Dropdown 2, for anyone not part of the original Circles (e.g. a new contact who messaged while away) — added one at a time, not in bulk.

### Accidental-tap protection

Applies to any bulk action in Dropdown 1 (the shared "All" send, or a per-Circle send): a brief confirm step — "Send '[message]' to [N] people?" with Send / Cancel — before sending.

No counts, no unread badges, no "outstanding" language anywhere in this screen. "Hold remembers where you left off. Continue whenever you're ready."

## Text input pattern: one docked bar, everywhere (supersedes per-screen in-page boxes)

**Every free-text field in the app — a message, a Circle name, an account nickname, a saved template, no exception carved out for something "too small to bother" — is entered and edited through one shared component, docked directly above the keyboard when open**, not an in-page text box living in the scrolling content. Reference: Instagram's own message bar, not the OS's native autocomplete row (an app can't inject content into system autocomplete — this had to be Hold's own built component). Supersedes every prior description of an in-page `TextInput` throughout this book; where earlier sections say "a text box" or "an editable field," read it as this pattern now.

- **Two states for any field:** not yet started shows its placeholder (e.g. "Message for Priya"); once something's been typed, it shows the current text plus a small "Edit" affordance. Tapping either state loads that field into the one docked bar, active and ready to type — the same interaction whether starting fresh or editing something that already exists, everywhere in the app.
- **New-Circle suggestion chips** ("Friends," "Work," "Book Club") lose their "+" prefix — name only. **Revised (2026-08-11):** now render as their own row directly above the docked bar's input line, not inline in Going Quiet's scrollable Circle row — visible only while the field is empty, tapping one submits immediately (same as typing the name and confirming), replaced by the bar itself once typing starts. Tapping "+" opens the docked bar immediately, focused and ready to type — no intermediate tap through a separate preview box first, which the original build of this pattern still had. **Corrected again, same day:** the suggestions themselves changed from circle-styled chips to a plain text list — words separated by a thin vertical divider line, in one horizontally scrollable row, at 16pt (matching the input's own font size, inside the documented 15-17pt Compact-text range) — circle-styled suggestion chips read as competing with the actual Circle chips they exist to help create, sitting directly below Going Quiet's own row of real ones.
- **Dismissing a field without committing (2026-08-11):** the old "+"-flow's separate Add/Cancel buttons are gone. The bar's own paper-plane commits (`onDone`); a new, generic dismiss path — wired to the keyboard's own hide event, and also reachable via the existing tap-outside-to-dismiss gesture every screen already has — closes the bar without committing. Fields that need dismiss-without-committing behaviour (new-Circle naming, so a half-typed name never silently becomes a Circle) opt into their own dismiss handling; every other field defaults to the same "just close" behaviour it always had. This is a generic mechanism the bar provides to every screen, not a one-off fix for Going Quiet specifically.
- **One exception, deliberate: Personalise's "What they sent" box** stays an in-page field, not the docked bar. This is reference content pasted in once, not something actively composed — the person needs to scroll back and re-read it while writing their reply separately in the docked bar, without the friend's own words risking an accidental edit. Settles into a muted, read-only-looking card once populated (the same desaturated treatment as an existing sent-state pill), with an explicit "Edit" control — no hidden gesture, and no risk of it being mistaken for something the person is meant to write themselves.
- **Dictation:** a mic in the docked bar, using the platform's own on-device speech recognition (iOS's SFSpeechRecognizer, Android's SpeechRecognizer, forced to on-device-only rather than the cloud-accuracy default) — nothing spoken leaves the device for this feature, consistent with Hold's privacy stance elsewhere. Icon enlarged (2026-08-11, on-device feedback) for easier targeting.
- **One trailing action icon, always** (2026-08-11, confirmed by direct instruction after briefly considering a context-aware checkmark variant and explicitly rejecting it): the docked bar's confirm/complete action is always the same paper-plane glyph already established on `CompactSendButton`, in every context — a Circle name, an account label, a message, all the same icon. No second icon for non-message contexts.
- **Visual treatment, corrected three times the same day (2026-08-11):** an earlier pass on this same date gave the bar a thicker, darker-green top border — on-device, that read as a jarring, heavy line, and it's removed entirely, replaced with rounded top corners (24pt at first, a reasoned choice approximating a soft merge into the keyboard's own top edge, not a measured value). **Reduced a third time, same day, to 10pt:** 24pt read as too aggressively curved on-device and visually clipped against the AI-amend/send icons near the bar's own top corners — 10pt keeps a subtler rounded-edge treatment closer to the keyboard's own corner style, with the pill's own side padding widened slightly for extra clearance around those icons. Applies everywhere this bar appears, not just Going Quiet/Reconnect. Shape restructured Instagram-style: the mic, send, **and Amend-with-AI icons** all sit inside the text field's own rounded pill as one continuous shape, not separate elements in a row — Amend-with-AI moved in from outside the pill in the second same-day correction below, after an on-device pass found it sitting apart from the other two; the sparkle/star icon itself is unchanged, kept as the established cross-industry "AI action" convention. **Colour, corrected a second time same day:** the first correction's blend (existing chip-green blend inside the pill, app-palette-leaning blend outside it) still read as uniformly too dark on-device, especially in dark mode, where both source tokens were themselves dark, leaving no visible lightness contrast between the pill and its surround. Asked to sample Instagram's own actual composer colours directly rather than continue approximating — this remains genuinely unresolved: there's no way to open Instagram or read real device pixels from this environment, and a web search for the values surfaced only unverified fan-made clone projects, not a credible source, so it was flagged rather than treated as a real match. In its place: a reasoned, principled adjustment targeting the two specific complaints — inside the pill, light mode now leans the blend toward white (an existing theme token), dark mode leans it toward the app's own vivid accent green rather than the dark tokens used before, so the pill reads as a clearly lighter accent against a darker surround (the relationship Instagram's own dark-mode composer has to its background, which the first correction had inverted); outside the pill, the blend leans slightly further toward the approximated keyboard grey. Recomputed contrast (pill fill against the theme's own text colour): light-normal ≈12.8:1 (up from ≈9.55:1); dark-normal ≈6.0:1 (down from ≈9.19:1) — still comfortably past WCAG AA's 4.5:1, but a real, reported trade-off of deliberately lightening the dark-mode pill, not one absorbed silently. Both the keyboard-grey approximation and the corner radius remain unverified against a real device — confirm on-device. **Icon position, corrected the same day, later still (2026-08-11):** the mic/send/Amend-with-AI icons had settled to the *bottom* of the pill, where the outer two clipped against the pill's own rounded corner on a tall (multi-line) box. Moved to the top instead, following the curve of the pill's rounded top edge, with more generous top/side padding for clearance.
- **Flush-to-keyboard positioning (2026-08-11):** a real, structural gap between the docked bar and the keyboard's top edge — separate from the colour/border work above, and left open through an earlier pass that only addressed colour — was root-caused to the bar's keyboard-following wrapper being nested inside a bottom-inset safe-area container, which shifted its resting position away from the true screen edge by the home-indicator's own clearance without the keyboard-follow logic knowing to compensate. Fixed structurally, not by adding padding to bridge the gap, by removing that nesting entirely.

### Template insertion and sentence-suggestion pills, 2026-08-13

**Two genuinely different boxes are in play here, not one thing tested twice — worth being explicit about which is which.** "Box A" is the resting/static preview shown before tapping in (`DockedFieldPreview` — plain text plus its own pill row, no live input). "Box B" is the keyboard-accessory bar that appears once you tap in to actually type (`DockedInputBar` — where the green-highlight mechanic itself lives). They're built and fixed somewhat independently below, since a bug confirmed on one doesn't mean the other has it too.

**"Template"/"Remove template" button, directly beneath the message box (Box B), part of the docked bar itself — not a separate row stacked above it.** Corrected 2026-08-13: on-device it originally rendered grouped with the AI-amend row/sentence pills, all positioned above the actual message box, which read as a standalone floating banner rather than part of the input; moved to sit directly under the box instead. **Also on Box A now, as of the same day's second pass** — the static preview had no Template affordance at all, a real gap, not just a positioning issue; added as a plain (non-green-tracked) insert, matching how Box A's own pills already work. Present only where a caller has a Circle's own saved default to offer. Tapping it inserts that text alongside/below whatever's already there — never a destructive replace — rendering green to distinguish it from the person's own words. Editing *anywhere* inside that green block reverts the *whole* block to normal text immediately, not just the touched part — no partial/word-level tracking. Once reverted, it's no longer removable as a single unit; the person deletes manually from there, same as editing their own writing. The label permanently changes from "Template" to "Remove template" the first time it's ever inserted this compose session (closed-book icon → open-book icon, text label primary, icon secondary, per the app's established accessibility labelling rule) — it does not revert back to "Template" if the block later reverts by editing; "Remove template" simply becomes unavailable/greyed at that point. Sized tight around their own text — no excess padding stretching them into ovals/bars, matching the app's true-circle/pill sizing discipline elsewhere. **The inline preview box (`DockedFieldPreview` — the tap-to-open trigger shown before the docked bar is active) needed its own separate pill row, added explicitly** — it's a different component from the docked bar and doesn't inherit the row automatically; tapping a pill there inserts plain text (not green-tracked, since the docked bar itself isn't mounted yet at that moment) and opens the bar. **Not yet done: threading this into every `DockedFieldPreview` call site app-wide** — built for Going Quiet's and Reconnect's own message fields (the two most central compose surfaces) first; several other call sites (Library's per-person fields, Manage Circles, email out-of-office, wider-world status, Personalise's reply box) don't have it yet. **Save to Library, mirrored onto Box B (2026-08-13):** was present on Box A's own preview but missing entirely once the bar was actually open — added left of Template in the same footer row, same left/right convention as Box A. **Re-reported missing on both boxes on the very next on-device pass — not yet confirmed as a real bug.** No code fault found on re-reading either box's gating; the leading, unconfirmed hypothesis is that Save-to-Library has *never* existed for a multi-Circle combination selection (Template has, since it always supported combinations) and the person may have been testing with one selected — logged as an open question pending confirmation of the exact selection tested, not silently assumed fixed or silently assumed still broken.

**Box A's own scroll problem — two distinct bugs, found across two passes.** First: no height cap at all — long saved text just kept extending. Fixed with a plain scroll container, capped ~5 lines. **Second, found on the very next test: it still didn't scroll at all, a genuinely different bug from the first fix not actually working.** Root cause: the scroll container had been placed *inside* the same `Pressable` that opens the docked bar on tap — and that `Pressable` itself already sits inside the screen's own outer scroll. A scrollable area nested inside a tappable area nested inside another scrollable area is a well-known mobile gesture conflict, where the outermost one generally wins the touch before the innermost one ever gets to recognise a drag as scrolling rather than a tap. Fixed by no longer wrapping the scrollable content in the tap-to-open element at all — the existing "Edit" link is now the only thing that opens the bar once there's content; scrolling the box itself no longer competes with anything.

**Pill tap on Box A wrongly popping the keyboard open, found the same pass — fixed.** Tapping a suggestion pill was deliberately built to insert text *and* open the docked bar together, earlier in this same day's work. Reversed: pills on Box A now insert only: the wording lands in the box, reviewable by scrolling it (now actually working), without forcing the keyboard up as a side effect. Opening the bar stays a separate, deliberate action.

**Sentence-pill corners softened.** Was using the same full-stadium rounding as a true Circle chip, which read as visibly clipping the pill's own text at that curvature. Reduced to a gentle radius matching the docked bar's own corner rounding — scoped to sentence-suggestion pills specifically; Circle-selection pills/chips elsewhere are unrelated and unchanged.

**Box B's scroll: confirmed genuinely working on-device for the first time this session, then extended.** The rebuilt shared-scroll-container architecture (described above) held up under real testing — a solid foundation the same day's further changes build on, not another attempt that also failed. Extended per direct instruction: 5 lines → 7 (both the input's own cap and the surrounding pill shape's own height ceiling, which would otherwise have silently clipped the taller box even with the input itself sized correctly). Manual drag-scrolling through everything typed so far was already unconditionally available and needed no change — only *typing* triggers the auto-follow-to-the-latest-line behaviour, so a manual scroll away from the bottom to review earlier content is never fought while not actively composing. **The known limitation from before still stands, restated rather than solved in this pass:** auto-follow always jumps to the very end on any change, which is right for composing forward or resuming after a review-scroll, but would still incorrectly jump to the end if someone manually scrolled up and typed an edit into the *middle* of existing text — genuinely fixing that needs the cursor's real position, deliberately not added on top of a mechanism that had only just been confirmed stable, rather than risk it in the same pass that also changed the line count.

## Amend with AI (Hold+) — now lives inside the docked bar itself, not a separate panel

**Supersedes the standalone panel described in earlier revisions of this section** (a link below the message box that expanded into its own prompt-and-generate panel). The AI-assist trigger is now a button built into the one shared docked bar, available wherever that bar has message-shaped content open — Going Quiet's per-Circle box, Reconnect's box, Taking Time's "Send an update" box, Personalise/Conversations' reply box, and now also email out-of-office, wider-world status, and Library's saved templates, which didn't have Amend with AI at all before this pass.

- **Trigger and panel:** an icon in the docked bar; tapping it reveals a small inline prompt, "Add context for AI, or leave blank," with its own compact Generate action, directly in the bar rather than a separate section of the page. Generating blends whatever's currently in the field with the typed context — the model changes only what the new context requires and keeps the rest, never a full rewrite. "Done" (the bar's own) applies the result and returns to normal typing.
- **Always present regardless of Hold+ status:** manual typing in the docked bar itself. Amend with AI is additive; the trigger is absent entirely for free users (not greyed out or locked), never the only way to change a message.
- **Personalise's pinned "What they sent" box** is unaffected by this move — see the text-input pattern above; it was never an Amend-with-AI surface to begin with.

### AI memory — a separate, further opt-in

Two layers, both required before anything is captured or shown:

- **Layer 1:** a standing toggle, "Remember helpful details for drafting and notifications," off by default, set ahead of time in the Hold+ area — never offered as an in-the-moment prompt during Going Quiet or Reconnect.
- **Layer 2:** only if Layer 1 is on, the same Amend-with-AI request may also ask the model to quietly note a short, relevant detail — no interruption at the moment of drafting. Later, during Taking Time or Reconnect specifically (never Going Quiet), the oldest unused note surfaces as a dismissible suggestion: "Use it" feeds it straight into that screen's own docked bar, pre-filled and already open (kept afterward, but not suggested again); "Don't remember" deletes it outright. If Layer 1 is off, Layer 2 never surfaces anything, and turning Layer 1 off deletes every note already stored.
- **Retention:** 90 days from creation, confirmed as a standalone figure rather than tied to any trial or subscription mechanism — long enough to cover a real illness cycle end-to-end, short enough to avoid indefinite retention of personal details. See `docs/03-privacy-model.md` (in `hold-app`), "AI memory."
- **Not included:** using this data for Patterns/analytics — drafting-only for now; that would need its own separate disclosure and decision.

The Hold+ info screen carries a fixed, illustrative before/after example of Amend with AI (clearly labelled as a static example, not a live preview) alongside its existing feature list.

## Technical wording rule

Native share sheets and non-SMS channels may not confirm delivery. Use "sent" only when the platform can truthfully confirm sending. For non-SMS channels, Hold prepares the message and hands off to the relevant share/app flow rather than claiming to send directly.
